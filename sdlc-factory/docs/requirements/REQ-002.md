# REQ-002 — Proof of control lets a domain's owner correct the report's market or have the report taken down

- Priority: Must

## User story
As the owner of a business somebody else scanned — and as a founder whose own
site was read as the wrong kind of business — I want the report to tell me how to
prove I control the domain and to let me either correct the market it measured me
in or have it taken down, so that a permanent public verdict about my business is
either right or gone.

## Rationale
The market is inferred from a homepage, so when the inference is wrong every
number under it is wrong too, and the report is a public verdict about somebody
else's website: whoever controls the domain must be able to see which market it
was measured in, correct it, or have the report removed, on a proof only they can
produce, and no stranger may displace what they set. Correction and removal are
the two dispositions of that one proof — the same authority, decided at the last
step — and a report is taken down only on a request made this way, never at
ReachKit's own initiative. Which kind of mail carries the confirmation link is
REQ-064's. Why the free report allows exactly one correction to anyone who has
not proved control is a cost bound, held in `registry/evidence/REQ-014.md`.
Placement: JN-006 steps 2 to 5, and JN-001 step 6. Sources: `BUILD.md` §3 (the
public report path), §4.1 (header module) and §6.7 step 5. No implementation
exists to touch: this repo carries no `src/`.

## Acceptance criteria
1. Given any report, when it renders, then it names how to prove control of that
   domain and what proving it allows — correcting the market the report measured
   (criterion 9) or having the report removed: the exact DNS TXT record to
   publish for that domain, the mailbox at that domain criterion 2's link would
   be sent to, and the address a request is sent to — all readable by a visitor
   with no account, session, or payment.
2. Given a request that only whoever controls a domain may make — removing its
   report, correcting it as the domain's owner (criterion 9), or making a
   removed domain scannable again — when it is judged, then it completes only on
   proof that the requester controls that domain: either the DNS TXT record
   criterion 1 named is observed published on that domain, or ReachKit sends a
   link to an address at that domain and that link is used, that address being
   the one criterion 1 named and never one the requester chooses. The address a
   request arrives from never proves control by itself. Proof once completed is
   a standing and not a single act: for 30 days afterwards the person who
   produced it may make any further request of this kind for that domain without
   proving control again, and every report of that domain they open in that time
   offers them what a proven owner may do (criterion 4); after 30 days
   the standing lapses and either route above restores it. The mail is sent once
   per request either way — a standing that persists is not a mail that repeats:
   that link is a mail the recipient cannot stop, sent only in answer to a
   request they made and never again unprompted.
3. Given a removal request completed under criterion 2, when it is granted, then
   within 5 working days the report is no longer readable at its address, and
   that address shows the removed report to nobody and starts no scan for anyone,
   on that visit or any later one — until a request completed the same way asks
   for that domain to be scannable again. In its place, one written line says the
   report was removed at the domain owner's request and names the address such a
   request is sent to, so the owner who changes their mind has the route back
   where they will look for it. No address is withheld this way for a domain
   whose owner has made no such request: a report is taken down only by a
   removal request completed under criterion 2, never at ReachKit's own
   initiative.
4. Given any completed report, when it renders, then the category the report
   measured is shown, together with a control to correct it — on every report,
   not only where the market looks wrong. On a domain whose owner has set its
   category (criterion 9) the category is still shown, but no correction control
   is offered to anyone who has not proved control of the domain; one written
   line says the domain's owner set it and points at the proof of control
   criteria 1 and 2 name.
5. Given a report whose twelve searches share no common top-ten domain across at
   least three of them, when it renders, then one written line states that the
   market may have been read wrongly and points at that control.
6. Given a founder who submits a corrected category, when the correction is
   accepted, then the market set, the twelve questions and the report's
   measurements are re-derived from the corrected category and the report
   re-renders from them.
7. Given a corrected report, when any later visitor opens that domain's report
   address, then they see the corrected report — the correction replaces the one
   stored report for that domain (REQ-001 criterion 12).
8. Given a report whose one correction has already been used, when any visitor
   attempts a further correction, then they are told in writing that a free scan
   allows one correction and that this one has been used, and the corrected
   report stays readable; where two corrections are submitted before either has
   been applied, the first accepted becomes the stored report and every other is
   refused the same way.
9. Given a visitor who has proved control of the domain in the way criterion 2
   names — a correction being one of the requests that criterion covers, needing
   no removal to be asked for — when they submit a correction, then it is applied
   and becomes the stored report even where the scan's one correction was already
   used by someone else; that category then holds for that domain's free reports
   — every later free scan of it is measured against that category — and no
   correction by anyone who has not proved control of the domain is accepted for
   it again, on that scan or any later one, until someone who has proved control
   submits another. It binds the free path and nothing else: the market a paying
   account is measured in is the one its founder confirms at setup (REQ-026) and
   may replace afterwards (REQ-071 criterion 1), which no proof of control over a
   public report displaces or is displaced by.
10. Given a domain re-scanned into a new stored report, when that report is
    opened, then one correction is available again where the re-scan was offered
    because the previous report was 7 days old or older (REQ-001 criterion 15),
    and not where it was offered because that report was incomplete (REQ-001
    criterion 14) — a re-measurement of an incomplete report carries the
    correction already spent on it, so re-scanning is never a way to buy a
    further one. Where someone who proved control has set the domain's category
    (criterion 9), no correction is offered to anyone who has not, on any later
    free scan.

## Non-goals
- No ban on a domain its owner has not asked to remove (criterion 3), and no
  route back other than the one criterion 3 names — removal is lifted only by
  the same proof of control that obtained it.
- No second proof of control elsewhere in the product: criterion 2 is the single
  home for what proving control of a domain means.
- Which mailbox at a domain criterion 2's link is sent to: a membership set the
  blueprint holds. This requirement promises only that the requester never
  chooses it and that the report names it before it is used.
- No editing of individual questions, searches or rivals on the free report.
- No repeated or unlimited corrections on the free path for anyone who has not
  proved control: one accepted correction per free scan, and after a proven
  owner has set the category, none.
- No confirming or freezing of the market on the free path; the category is
  confirmed after payment, at setup (`BUILD.md` §6.7 step 5).
- The full enumeration of which re-scan routes mint a fresh correction: the
  blueprint's, derived from criterion 10's promise that ageing past 7 days does
  and re-measuring an incomplete report does not.
