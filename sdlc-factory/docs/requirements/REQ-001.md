# REQ-001 — One field starts a free scan, and `/scan/{domain}` is the one public address that serves it

- Priority: Must

## User story
As a founder who has never heard of ReachKit — or a colleague they sent the link
to — I want one field and one address to give me a report about a domain, so that
I can find out how findable I am without creating an account, paying, or learning
anything first, and can come back to what I already have.

## Rationale
A stranger must be able to reach a verdict about a domain from one field with
nothing asked of them first, land on the single public report address for that
domain however they typed it, and find that address still serving the same
report, dated, whenever they or anyone holding the link return — a barrier, a
second address, a blank page or a silent re-measurement each end the funnel at
the point it is met. This requirement is the single home for what
`/scan/{domain}` serves, to whom, and when a scan starts, and it owns the first
visit to a report address for an unscanned domain whether that visit came from
the landing field, from a link somebody shared, or from an address typed by hand
(criterion 9). Placement: JN-001 steps 1 and 2, and JN-006 step 1. Sources:
`BUILD.md` §3 (landing, the `/scan/{domain}` route contract, the public report
path), §4.1 (header module, "Copy link") and States (cooldown), and §6.4 (the
7-day free re-scan window and the 24h failure cooldown). No implementation
exists to touch: this repo carries no `src/`.

## Acceptance criteria
1. Given a visitor on the landing page, when the page renders, then it presents
   exactly one text input and one submit control and no other input control of
   any kind — no market, competitor, keyword or country field, selector or
   toggle, optional or required — and no sign-in or payment is required before
   submitting.
2. Given a visitor who enters a domain in any of its written forms — with or
   without a scheme, with or without `www`, with a trailing path, in any letter
   case — when they submit, then they arrive at the single public report address
   for that domain; no two of those forms produce different addresses or
   different stored reports.
3. Given a visitor who submits a value that is not a well-formed public domain
   name — an empty value, an IP literal, or a hostname with no public suffix —
   when they submit, then one written line names what is wrong with the value
   and the visitor stays on the landing page with their input intact. A
   well-formed domain is accepted here whether or not it resolves; a domain that
   cannot be reached is a failed scan (REQ-003), not a rejected input.
4. Given a report address whose domain segment is not a well-formed public
   domain name, when it is opened, then one written line names what is wrong
   with it and the visitor is offered the landing field — never a blank page, an
   unhandled error, or a scan.
5. Given a report address for a well-formed domain, when a visitor opens it
   directly, then what loads is always one of three readable things — a stored
   report, a scan in progress, or one written line saying why there is no report
   for them to read — and never a blank page, a 404, or an unhandled error.
6. Given the report address for a domain, when a visitor with no session, no
   cookie, and no prior visit opens it, then the full report is readable without
   signing in or paying.
7. Given a visitor on a completed report, when they use the copy-link control,
   then the copied address renders the same report, with the same measurements
   and the same measurement date, for a different person on a different device.
8. Given any report address, when a search engine or other crawler requests it,
   then the page tells it not to index the page, and no sitemap the product
   publishes lists any report address; the report stays reachable by its link to
   anyone who has it.
9. Given a well-formed domain that has no stored report, is not in a failure
   cooldown, and whose report was not removed at its owner's request, when its
   report address is first reached — by a submission on the landing page, or by
   opening that address directly, whether the visitor typed it by hand or
   followed a link somebody shared — then a scan for that domain is already
   underway with no further action from the visitor (a domain already measured
   or in cooldown is criteria 12 to 16's; a removed report is REQ-002's, and a
   visitor who has met a free-path limit is REQ-003's).
10. Given a visitor for whom a scan started under criterion 9, when they wait
    through it and read the report, then nothing is asked of them at any point
    between the domain they gave and the report they read — no account, sign-in,
    email address, or payment, and no further field. The finished page they may
    trade an address for afterwards is REQ-010's.
11. Given a stored report that no removal request under REQ-002 criterion 2 has
    removed, when its address is opened at any later time by anyone holding the
    link,
    then the same report is still readable there under the same address,
    carrying the measurement date it was stored with. No report link expires, is
    bound to the visitor who first opened it, or stops resolving with age; a
    stored report ageing only gains the re-scan control criteria 14 and 15
    govern.
12. Given a domain whose scan produced a stored report less than 7 days ago —
    whether it measured everything or only part of it (REQ-004) — when anyone
    opens its report address, then that stored report is shown with the date it
    was measured and no new measurement runs.
13. Given a stored report less than 7 days old that measured everything it set
    out to, when it is shown, then no re-scan control is offered.
14. Given a stored report that is incomplete — because part of it could not be
    measured (REQ-004), or because the scan stopped early at the 90-second
    ceiling (REQ-003 criterion 5) or the free path's spend ceiling (REQ-003
    criterion 11) — when it is opened at any age, then one written line says it
    is incomplete and a re-scan control is offered, and the stored report stays
    readable until the visitor chooses to re-scan.
15. Given a stored report 7 days old or older, when it is opened, then a re-scan
    control is offered and the stored report stays readable until the visitor
    chooses to re-scan.
16. Given a domain whose scan failed without producing a report less than 24
    hours ago, when its report address is opened, then one written line says what
    happened and a manual retry control is offered, and no scan starts by itself.
17. Given a visitor who starts a re-scan, when it runs, then the stored report
    and its measurement date stay readable until a new report replaces them, and
    if the re-scan does not complete the previous report remains at that address.
18. Given a domain whose report was removed at its owner's request (REQ-002
    criterion 3), when its report address is opened, then no stored report is
    shown, no re-scan or retry control is offered, and no scan starts — criteria
    12 to 17 offer no route back to a removed report.

## Non-goals
- No account, email address, or payment before a report is produced — stated as
  criterion 10, not withheld here.
- No market, competitor, keyword, or country input on the landing page
  (criterion 1) — the market is derived and corrected on the report itself
  (REQ-094).
- No second scan type, tier selector, or engine setting (`BUILD.md` §17,
  "Settings that tune the engine").
- No private, per-visitor, or expiring report links — permanence is stated as
  criterion 11, not left to this list.
- No editing, annotating, or branding of a report by whoever shares it.
- No account, session, or email gate on any part of the report — the only trade
  is the finished page (REQ-010).
- No scheduled or automatic re-measurement of a free report.
- No notification, email, or badge when a stored report becomes stale.
- No history of previous free reports for the same domain; one stored report per
  domain.
- The exhaustive mapping of every state a report address can be in — no stored
  report, failure cooldown, removed, free-path limit met — to what loads there:
  the blueprint's, derived from criterion 5's promise that a report address
  never ends in a blank page, a 404 or an unhandled error, from criteria 9 and
  12 to 18, and from REQ-002 and REQ-003, which own removal and the free path's
  limits.
