# REQ-099 — The landing page carries the whole pitch — the first screen, then the demo, then why · what · how to start

- Priority: Must

## User story
As a stranger who has just clicked a link to ReachKit, I want the first screen to
tell me what this is and show me what it looks like, and the page below it to walk
me from why I should care to what I do today, so that I can decide whether to type
my domain in instead of guessing what I would be typing it into.

## Rationale
The landing page is the first touchpoint anyone has with the product, and what
must be true is that a visitor can see, without scrolling, what ReachKit is and
what it looks like, and can then read themselves through why they should care,
what it does for them, and how to start. The owner's statement of 2026-09-02,
what it supersedes in `BUILD.md` §3, the two readings of REQ-001 criterion 1, and
every choice taken under rule 1.1 are in `registry/evidence/REQ-099.md`. This
requirement declares `blocked-by: [REQ-001]` (rule 2.3): that approved criterion
reads "exactly one text input and one submit control and no other input control
of any kind", and whether the ban is bounded by the scan-parameter list that
follows it or reaches every control on the page is the first open question below —
the video's play control and criterion 3's repeated calls to action stand or fall
on the answer. Criterion 1 is the small-screen criterion ADR-093's Consequences
record as owed by the analyst, and it is written against that decision's own
bands and 320 px floor rather than against "any device". Placement: JN-001 gains
a first step for it, and its domain-typing step gains this requirement — JN-001
was `approved` and dropped to `in-review` in the same edit. What it reaches: no
code — this repo carries no `src/` — but two approved blueprints publishing the
landing contract, BP-022 (`src/app/(public)/page.tsx`, "landing: one text input,
one submit, nothing else") and BP-001 ("landing: one field, one submit"), and
ADR-093, whose conformance suite asserts nothing about height and so cannot yet
fail criterion 1; each is named with its clause in the evidence file.

## Acceptance criteria
1. Given a visitor arriving at the landing address on any supported viewport —
   320 CSS px wide and up (ADR-093 Decision 2) — when the page first paints and
   before they scroll, then the tagline, one subline beneath it, and REQ-001
   criterion 1's one text input with its submit control are all entirely within
   that first screen, none of them clipped, truncated or set below the type floor;
   and at ADR-093's `medium` and `wide` bands one component showing what the
   product itself looks like is entirely within it as well. At `compact` that
   component is the one of the four that yields — it renders below the first
   screen, in normal flow — and nothing else is moved, dropped or made smaller to
   keep it above (ADR-093 Decision 3).
2. Given the tagline, when it renders, then it reads, verbatim: "See what AI
   tells buyers about your market — and write your way in."
3. Given the whole page, when a visitor counts what they can type into and what
   they can submit, then there is still exactly one of each (REQ-001 criterion 1),
   and every further call to action anywhere on the page brings that one field
   into view with the cursor in it, ready to type, without a page load and without
   adding a second input or a second submit control.
4. Given the component of criterion 1, when a visitor reads it, then it shows the
   product's own running interface — the screens a customer actually uses —
   rather than a stock illustration, a photograph, or a mock-up of a product that
   does not exist.
5. Given a visitor who scrolls past the first screen, when they continue, then
   the next thing on the page is a video showing the product being used — not a
   title card, a logo animation, a stock clip, or a sequence of still images.
6. Given the video does not play — absent, never produced, blocked, still
   loading, or a connection too slow for it — when the visitor continues, then the
   page still states everything criteria 1, 3, 7 and 8 require, a scan can still
   be started, and one written line stands where the video would be; no call to
   action and nothing on the first screen depends on the video having played.
7. Given the page below the first screen, when a visitor reads down it, then it
   answers three questions in this order, each in its own section under its own
   heading — why they should care, what ReachKit does for them, and what they do
   to start today — and that order holds unchanged at each of ADR-093's three
   bands. Where criterion 5's video is present it sits above those three sections;
   where it is not, they follow the first screen directly.
8. Given any number, score, band word, domain or brand name shown inside
   criterion 4's component or criterion 5's video, when a visitor reads it, then
   either it was read from a measurement the product stored for the domain shown
   and is shown with the date of the scan it came from (REQ-093 criterion 4), or
   it is an example about a reserved domain that is no real business and the
   surface says so; and in neither case is any figure shown about the visitor's own
   site, which nothing has measured yet.

## Non-goals
- What starting a scan does, what a valid domain is, what address the visitor
  lands on and what they meet afterwards — REQ-001.
- Every customer-visible string on this page except the tagline: those are the
  owner's and are enumerated as owed below, not drafted here.
- Producing, hosting, captioning or transcribing the demo video, and whether it
  autoplays — open below; criteria 6 and 7 fix only that the page works and keeps
  its order without it.
- Whether any sentence on this page may be model-written — REQ-093 is the single
  home for that rule and it holds here unchanged, including the video's captions,
  on-screen text and narration: REQ-093 criterion 1 binds "every text the product
  renders or sends, on every surface it speaks on … including surfaces added
  after this requirement".
- The sign-in screen a returning customer needs — REQ-098.
- A price surface with no report behind it, and what any offer to subscribe must
  state — REQ-021 criteria 1 to 4, REQ-022 criterion 1.
- Breakpoint values, image treatment, and the height of the first screen at each
  band — the design system's (`00-project.md`, standing law 1; ADR-093's three
  constraints). Criterion 1 fixes what must be in it, never how tall it is.
- A second landing variant, personalisation, or an A/B test.
- Marketing pages beyond this one: no blog, no case studies, no about page.

## Open questions
- [ ] REQ-001 criterion 1 reads "exactly one text input and one submit control
      and no other input control of any kind — no market, competitor, keyword or
      country field, selector or toggle". Two readings, and only the owner can say
      which was promised. **Bounded:** the list is the ban, so the promise is
      about the scan form — a video's play control and a repeated call to action
      are neither inputs nor submits and criteria 3 and 5 stand. **Whole page:**
      "of any kind" is the ban and the list illustrates it, so the promise is that
      the landing page carries no control but those two — criterion 5's video
      control and criterion 3's repeated calls to action break an approved
      promise and come out. BP-022 and BP-001 publish the same contract as the
      page's shape, so the answer moves them too. (owner: user)
- [ ] The demo video is an asset class this corpus has no home for: who produces
      it, where it is hosted, and whether it autoplays. No `BUILD.md` §16
      milestone names it and no cost-envelope row covers it. (owner: user)
- [ ] The customer-visible strings this page needs, none of which the system may
      write (§1, decision-rights table). The tagline in criterion 2 stands; these
      are owed:
      1. the subline beneath the tagline (criterion 1);
      2. the label on the submit control that starts the scan (criterion 1);
      3. the label on every further call to action lower down the page
         (criterion 3);
      4. the heading and body of the "why should they care" section (criterion 7);
      5. the heading and body of the "what it does for them" section
         (criterion 7);
      6. the heading and body of the "what they do to start today" section
         (criterion 7);
      7. any caption or label on the product component (criterion 4);
      8. any heading or caption on the video section (criterion 5);
      9. the line that stands where the video would be when it will not play
         (criterion 6);
      10. any interface copy visible inside criterion 4's component: where it is
          the product's own registered strings it is already the owner's (BP-019),
          and anything the component shows that the product does not say elsewhere
          is new copy on a public page;
      11. where criterion 8's second branch is used, the line saying a figure is
          an example.
      (owner: user)
- [ ] REVIEW(untestable: criterion 1): "entirely within that first screen" fixes
      no height, and nothing this criterion cites fixes one either — ADR-093
      Decision 2 commits a width range ("320 CSS px and up"), Decision 6 renders
      each route at "five widths" and asserts nothing about height, and this
      file's own non-goal hands "the height of the first screen at each band" to
      the design system. A tall enough viewport passes the criterion at every
      width and a short enough one fails it at every width, so no reviewer can
      name the viewport at which this failed. The rewrite moved the
      untestability from "any device" to the vertical axis rather than removing
      it; the rationale concedes the outcome in terms — the suite "cannot yet
      fail criterion 1".
- [ ] REVIEW(conflict with ADR-093 Decision 3): criterion 1's "nothing else is
      moved, dropped or made smaller to keep it above (ADR-093 Decision 3)"
      forbids what Decision 3 prescribes. Decision 3's remedy is that a surface
      that cannot carry its content "renders a different registered state —
      fewer elements, or a narrower registered variant"; what it bans is "the
      same elements smaller" below the floor, which criterion 1's first sentence
      already covers with "set below the type floor". As written the criterion
      cites the decision for a rule it does not contain and forecloses the one it
      does: a compact arm that drops the subline into a registered narrower state
      fails criterion 1 and passes ADR-093.
- [ ] REVIEW(conflict with this file's own non-goals): criterion 1 picks which of
      the four items reflows at one named band ("that component is the one of the
      four that yields"), while the non-goal list says "Criterion 1 fixes what
      must be in it, never how tall it is" and gives band behaviour to the design
      system. Which element leaves the first screen at `compact` is a band-arm
      choice — ADR-093 Decision 6's required `arms` — which §4 seats with the
      design-guardian and architect. The owner is asked to approve a promise and
      a layout ruling in one criterion.
- [ ] REVIEW(conflict: criterion 5 against criteria 6 and 7): criterion 5 states
      without condition that "the next thing on the page is a video showing the
      product being used", while criterion 6 covers a video that is "absent,
      never produced", criterion 7 states the order for "where it is not" and
      JN-001's first step reads "watches the demo, or reads the page unbroken
      where there is none". A page shipped before the video exists — the state
      the `rests-on` row says holds today — fails criterion 5 while satisfying 6
      and 7, so the requirement cannot be met as a whole until an asset nothing
      in this corpus produces exists.
- [ ] REVIEW(conflict with REQ-001 criterion 1): the `blocked-by` edge is
      declared, but criteria 3 and 5 still assert one reading as settled —
      criterion 3 promises "every further call to action anywhere on the page
      brings that one field into view", which the evidence says "survives on the
      bounded reading and falls on the whole-page reading", and criterion 5
      requires a video whose play control the whole-page reading forbids.
      Approving this requirement as written rules open question 1 for the bounded
      reading without the owner ever answering it; neither criterion marks itself
      contingent on that ruling.
- [ ] REVIEW(gap: criteria 4 and 8): criterion 8 reaches "any number, score, band
      word, domain or brand name", but criterion 4's component "shows the
      product's own running interface", so it also carries sentences — headings,
      verdict lines, empty states. Criterion 4 bars only "a mock-up of a product
      that does not exist", so a mock-up of one that does is admissible, and an
      image of the interface is arguably not "text the product renders or sends"
      under REQ-093 criterion 1. Invented interface copy on this public page
      therefore fails no criterion: owed-strings item 10 records it as owed, and
      an open question binds nothing.
- [ ] REVIEW(conflict with REQ-093 criterion 4): criterion 8's second branch
      admits a figure with no stored measurement behind it — "an example about a
      reserved domain that is no real business and the surface says so" — while
      REQ-093 criterion 4, which criterion 8 cites for its first branch, admits
      no exemption at all ("never estimated, never recomputed for display").
      Whether REQ-093 criterion 4 reaches a marketing component decides which
      governs, and the two artifacts answer oppositely for the same figure.
- [ ] REVIEW(ambiguity: criterion 8): "the surface says so" reads two ways — the
      surface says the domain is an example, or the surface says the figures are
      not measurements of anything. The difference decides whether a score no
      scan produced may stand on ReachKit's own public page under an
      example-domain label.
- [ ] REVIEW(gap: owed strings): the video's own words — its on-screen text and
      its narration script — are customer-visible strings this page shows, and no
      item of the eleven covers them. Item 8 covers "any heading or caption on the
      video section"; the non-goal puts "captioning or transcribing the demo
      video" out of scope; and the REQ-093 non-goal rules only whether those words
      may be model-written, not who writes them. The list is presented as what is
      owed, so approving it as complete leaves the words a stranger actually hears
      with no author named.
- [ ] REVIEW(gap: criterion 1's Given): the Given is "when the page first paints
      and before they scroll", so the first screen carries no promise in the one
      state most likely to overflow it — REQ-001 criterion 3's rejected
      submission, where "one written line names what is wrong with the value and
      the visitor stays on the landing page with their input intact". At 320 px
      that line joins the four items criterion 1 requires and nothing says what
      gives way.
