# REQ-098 — The sign-in screen — one address, one link, and no form that opens an account

- Priority: Must

## User story
As a returning customer on a device with no session, I want one screen that asks
for the address I pay with and sends me a link, and that tells me plainly when a
link I am holding no longer works, so that a product with no password never
leaves me with nowhere to go.

## Rationale
A returning customer on a new device, one who has just signed out, and one
holding a link that has expired or been spent must all have one address to go to
that asks for the only credential this product has and says plainly that there is
no password and no signup — without it, `BUILD.md` §1's "magic-link auth" is a
promise with no surface behind it, and three approved criteria point at a screen
that does not exist: REQ-020 criteria 4 and 5, and REQ-077 criterion 5. The
owner's endorsement of 2026-09-02, the verbatim transcription every string in
criteria 2 and 4 is taken from, and the derivations taken under rule 1.1 are in
`registry/evidence/REQ-098.md`. Placement: JN-004 gains one step for it, between
"Moves the address they sign in with, or signs out" and "Takes everything they
have published away with them", and its last step gains this requirement — JN-004
was already `in-review`, so no approval was reopened. What it reaches: no code —
this repo carries no `src/` — but four approved blueprints and two work orders,
each named with its clause in the evidence file: BP-032 (`requestMagicLink` and
its three answers), BP-061 (`issueLink`, `redeemLink`, `signin.link_dead`, and
one live token per purpose), BP-001 (whose public route table declares no sign-in
route, and which WO-136 names as the route's owner) and BP-019 (the copy registry
holding every key the lines below are owed against).

## Acceptance criteria
1. Given a person with no session, when they reach ReachKit's sign-in address,
   then the screen presents one email input and one submit control and nothing
   else to fill in: no password field, no social sign-in, and no control that
   opens an account. It is where the product sends anyone signed out — after
   signing out (REQ-077 criterion 5) and from any setup or app URL opened without
   a session (REQ-020 criterion 5).
2. Given the screen, when it renders, then it carries these strings verbatim: the
   heading "Welcome back"; the body "Enter the email you paid with and we'll send
   you a sign-in link. No password — accounts are created by payment, never by a
   signup form."; the email input's placeholder "you@company.com"; the submit
   control's label "Send my link"; and beneath that control the line "New to
   ReachKit? Start a free scan →", whose "Start a free scan →" is a link, and
   following it reaches the landing page where a free scan is started (REQ-001).
3. Given a person who submits an address, when they submit, then they are
   answered in writing on the same screen without being sent anywhere: an address
   with an open account is sent a link — including one whose plan lapsed long ago
   (REQ-076 criterion 5) — and an address with none is answered on REQ-020
   criterion 4's terms. Nothing a visitor can observe beyond those words differs
   by address: the answer takes the same time and the same number of requests is
   allowed whichever it was, and nothing on the screen before a submission
   reveals whether any address has an account (REQ-020 criterion 5).
4. Given the screen, when it renders, then alongside criterion 2's strings it
   carries these verbatim: the heading "One number tells you how findable you
   are."; and on one card beneath it the label "Discoverability Score", the pill
   "+6 pts est." and the line "Hard to find — and we'll show you the fixes that
   move it.". That card also shows a domain, a numeral, "/100" and a progress bar,
   and states no figure about a website beyond the numeral, the "/100" and the
   pill.
5. Given every figure on that card — the numeral, the "/100", the pill, the bar
   and the band words — when a visitor reads them, then either every one of them
   was read from a measurement the product stored for the domain shown and the
   card states that domain with the date of the scan they came from (REQ-093
   criterion 4); or none of them is about any real website, the domain shown is a
   reserved example domain that resolves to no real site, and the card carries a
   written line saying it is an example. In neither case does this screen show a
   domain, score or band belonging to an identifiable customer: it renders to
   anyone, with no session.
6. Given a person who submits an empty value or one that is not a valid email
   address, when they submit, then no link is sent, one written line names what is
   wrong, and they stay on the screen with what they typed intact.
7. Given a person who opens a sign-in link that no longer works — expired,
   already used, or one this product never issued — when they open it, then they
   land on this screen and one written line tells them the link can no longer be
   used and that they may ask for another; that line and the time it takes are the
   same whichever of the three it was, so someone holding a link that is not
   theirs learns nothing about whether the address it was issued for has an
   account.
8. Given a person who asks for a link more than once, when they open one of the
   mails, then the most recently requested link signs them in and any earlier one
   is answered as criterion 7 requires, so nobody is left clicking a link that
   stopped working with nothing said about why.

## Non-goals
- What the emailed link does when it is used and where it lands the customer —
  REQ-024 criteria 1 and 4; criterion 7 owns only the case where it does nothing.
- Passwords, social sign-in, SSO, two-factor, or account recovery — REQ-077.
- What an address with no ReachKit account is told — REQ-020 criterion 4;
  criterion 3 routes to it and adds nothing to it.
- How long a link lives, how many may be requested, and what stops abuse of the
  request: criteria 7 and 8 own only what the customer is told when a link no
  longer works.
- Moving the address an account signs in with — REQ-077.
- The landing page the "Start a free scan →" link reaches — REQ-099.
- What the score is, how it is computed, what its band words mean, and on what
  scale — REQ-004.
- Layout, panel widths, the gradient and the glass card, and whether criteria 2
  and 4 render in one column or two at any of ADR-093's three bands: the design
  system's, built from named tokens and registered components (`00-project.md`,
  standing law 1). Both sets of strings are carried at every band.

## Open questions
- [ ] Criterion 4's card is labelled "Discoverability Score". Nothing in this
      corpus gives the number a customer-visible name: REQ-004 criterion 1 fixes
      "a single score on a 0–100 scale" and the four band words, and names the
      number nowhere. Is "Discoverability Score" the product's name for it on
      every surface — the report header, Overview, the Monday mail — or copy
      local to this screen? (owner: user)
- [ ] What criterion 4's card is: one fixed example, the same for every visitor,
      or a real scan shown with its domain and its measurement date. Criterion 5
      binds both honestly and each costs something only the owner can spend. A
      real scan puts a real business's score on a page anyone can open, and the
      transcribed pill "+6 pts est." cannot stay with it — an estimate is not a
      stored measurement, and REQ-004 criterion 12 admits "no estimate, no
      interpolated value, no default". An example keeps the pill and costs the
      transcribed domain, replaced by a reserved one, plus one more written line
      saying the card is an example. (owner: user)
- [ ] The written lines this screen owes, none of which the system may write (§1,
      decision-rights table). Criteria 2 and 4 are already the owner's,
      transcribed from the live page; these are written nowhere:
      1. what a person is told when a link has been sent (criterion 3);
      2. and 3. REQ-020 criterion 4's two lines — a payment held, and no account —
         already booked as owed by WO-127 against BP-019's `signin.payment_held`
         and `signin.no_account`;
      4. the line naming a missing or malformed address (criterion 6);
      5. the line for a link that no longer works (criterion 7), BP-019's
         `signin.link_dead`;
      6. where the answer to the question above is an example, the line saying so
         (criterion 5).
      (owner: user)
- [ ] REVIEW(conflict with WO-127): criterion 3 carves the wording out — "Nothing a
      visitor can observe beyond those words differs by address" — but approved
      WO-127 binds it. Its test-plan row for REQ-020 c5 names the test "› the three
      branches perform the same round trips and are indistinguishable in latency and
      shape", and its file plan reads "the three branches perform the same number of
      database round trips and return within the same measured band, so neither
      latency nor wording distinguishes them". The refusal's BP-032 leg holds — that
      bullet's "Neither branch reveals more, and neither is rate-limited differently
      from the other" is a two-place construction over `payment_held_account_opening`
      and `no_account`, so BP-032 does not forbid the wording distinction REQ-020 c4
      requires. Its WO-127 leg does not hold: WO-127 spans three branches and names
      wording. As it stands the test WO-127 declares would fail criterion 3, and
      nothing says which of the two is wrong.
- [ ] REVIEW(ambiguity): criterion 3's colon reads two ways — "Nothing a visitor can
      observe beyond those words differs by address: the answer takes the same time
      and the same number of requests is allowed whichever it was". Definition (only
      those two must match, so an HTTP status, a redirect, a response header, or
      whether the submit control disables may differ by address) or illustration
      (nothing observable may differ, of which those are two). Under the first
      reading the criterion permits the oracle it was rewritten to close.
- [ ] REVIEW(untestable: criterion 3): "the answer takes the same time" fixes no
      tolerance and no measurement point, and "the same number of requests is
      allowed" fixes no number — this file's own non-goal puts "how many may be
      requested, and what stops abuse of the request" elsewhere, and no artifact in
      the chain states it (BP-032 says only "neither is rate-limited differently from
      the other"; WO-127 says "the same measured band" and never names the band). A
      test cannot distinguish equal allowances from no limit at all.
- [ ] REVIEW(conflict with criterion 4): criterion 5's first branch now requires that
      "the card states that domain with the date of the scan they came from", but
      criterion 4 closes the card — it "states no figure about a website beyond the
      numeral, the "/100" and the pill". A scan date is a figure about that website
      and criterion 4's closed list has no room for it. The date was added to
      criterion 5 without widening criterion 4.
- [ ] REVIEW(conflict with REQ-093 criterion 4): approved REQ-093 c4 reads "every
      value inside it was read from a measurement the product stored for that domain,
      carrying the date of the scan it came from — never estimated, never recomputed
      for display", and REQ-093's non-goals carve out no surface and no example.
      Criterion 5's second branch admits a card of figures read from no measurement.
      Either this card is an explanation of "a number, verdict or reason" — and the
      second branch is not available — or it is not, and criterion 5's citation of
      REQ-093 c4 inside its first branch is misplaced. Only one can be true.
- [ ] REVIEW(gap): criterion 5's second branch owes "a written line saying it is an
      example", but nothing guarantees a reader meets it. The non-goal guarantees only
      that "Both sets of strings are carried at every band" — criteria 2's and 4's —
      and the example line is in neither set; it is item 6 of the third open question
      above, still unwritten. Nothing fixes its proximity to the numeral or its
      prominence, and nobody is named to judge whether the card still reads as a
      measurement. All three clauses hold for a card showing a numeral, "/100", a bar
      and band words with the example line rendered below the first screen at
      `compact`.
- [ ] REVIEW(untestable: criterion 5): "a reserved example domain that resolves to no
      real site" names no register — RFC 2606's `example.com`, the `.example`,
      `.invalid` and `.test` TLDs, and a domain the owner happens to hold all read as
      candidates — and "resolves to no real site" is a property of live DNS at the
      moment of the check rather than of the string on the card. A test has nothing to
      assert against and would pass or fail with the network.
- [ ] REVIEW(conflict with REQ-099 criterion 8): one rule, two homes, two wordings.
      Here: "the domain shown is a reserved example domain that resolves to no real
      site". There: "it is an example about a reserved domain that is no real business
      and the surface says so". A DNS property and an ownership property are different
      tests and will be built differently; neither file cites the other, and rule 2.4
      admits one home for the claim.
- [ ] REVIEW(ambiguity): criterion 7's second cause, "already used", does not say
      whether it reaches a link BP-061 spends without the customer ever opening it —
      "Issuing a new one spends the previous" (which is criterion 8's case) and, on a
      completed address change, "every unspent token for that user is marked `spent`".
      BP-061 returns one `reason: 'spent'` for all of them. If "already used" means
      redemption only, criterion 7's guarantee that the line and the time "are the same
      whichever of the three it was" does not reach criterion 8's link, and criterion
      8's routing to criterion 7 lands on nothing.
- [ ] REVIEW(conflict with BP-001): criterion 1 makes this screen a destination — "It
      is where the product sends anyone signed out … and from any setup or app URL
      opened without a session (REQ-020 criterion 5)" — while approved BP-001 answers
      in place: its `## Error & edge behavior` states "A signed-out request to
      `/setup` or `/app` asks for an address and says nothing about whether an account
      or a payment exists (REQ-020 criterion 5)", and its `Routes (public)` table
      lists no sign-in address. REQ-020 c5 permits either; criterion 1 chooses the
      redirect, and WO-136 puts "The sign-in route" out of its own scope.
- [ ] REVIEW(conflict with BP-061): criterion 8 states as a customer promise what
      BP-061 took under its own authority — its `## Error & edge behavior` reads
      "Rate limiting: at most one live token per `(user_id, purpose)`. Issuing a new
      one spends the previous. A customer who clicks "send me a link" three times has
      one working link — the newest", citing no requirement, and its decision 2 leans
      on it ("the mitigating control is single use plus one live token per purpose").
      Either the promise is this requirement's and BP-061 is bound by it rather than
      free to reprice it as a rate-limiting parameter, or it is BP-061's and criterion
      8 is the second copy rule 2.4 forbids.
- [ ] REVIEW(gap): criterion 8 states no fact about this screen — its Given is "a
      person who asks for a link more than once" and its Then is about which mailed
      link works — and it crosses both of this file's own non-goals: "criteria 7 and 8
      own only what the customer is told when a link no longer works" (criterion 8
      says which link works, not what is said) and "What the emailed link does when it
      is used … REQ-024 criteria 1 and 4" (criterion 8 says the newest one "signs them
      in"). Whether the link's lifecycle is this requirement's one behaviour or a
      second one arriving (rule 2.1) is unstated.
