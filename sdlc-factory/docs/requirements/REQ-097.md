# REQ-097 — Stripe owns billing end to end — ReachKit renders no billing value and sends no billing mail

- Priority: Must

## User story
As a paying customer, I want every part of paying — the card, the invoices, the
billing details and cancelling — to happen on Stripe's own surfaces and every
mail about a payment to come from Stripe, so that the company holding my billing
is the one showing it to me and ReachKit never shows me a figure that is out of
date or writes to me about a payment it did not take.

## Rationale
What must be true for the customer is that there is one place money lives and it
is not ReachKit: a billing figure ReachKit renders is a second copy of a fact
Stripe holds, and a second copy is what goes stale in front of a paying customer.
The owner's statement of 2026-09-02, the corpus clauses this requirement is cut
against, the backward pass and the choices taken under rule 1.1 are in
`registry/evidence/REQ-097.md`. It contradicts approved artifacts and declares
that as `blocked-by` rather than overwriting them (rule 2.3): **REQ-076 criteria
1, 3, 5 and 7** — the four billing values Settings shows, the screen shown at the
moment cancellation is confirmed, and the billing details Settings still offers
after the paid-through date — and the two approved blueprints that publish those
same behaviours as interfaces, **BP-060** (`billingSummary`'s `nextInvoice` and
`card`, and `cancelSubscription`, whose comment reads "Returns the exact date
access ends so the confirming screen can state it") and **BP-055** (the `ACTIONS`
tuple's `'invoices'` and `'cancel'`). REQ-070 is a `depends-on` and not a
conflict: its criteria 1 and 2 are ceilings — "nothing beyond this list is
offered" — and criterion 1 below offers strictly less than they permit. Both open
questions must be ruled before this or REQ-076's criteria can be approved.
Placement: JN-004 step 5. What it reaches: no code — this repo carries no `src/`
and the design preview app carries no billing surface — and BP-016's `MAIL_KINDS`
and BP-017's `portalLink` are confirmed rather than moved; each clause is cited in
the evidence file's backward pass.

## Acceptance criteria
1. Given a customer anywhere in ReachKit, when they look for anything to do with
   money — the card, the invoices, the billing address, the VAT number, or
   cancelling — then the only thing ReachKit offers for any of them is a control
   that takes the customer to Stripe's own billing surface, and every such control
   anywhere in the product takes them to that same one destination: ReachKit
   offers no separate control per item and presents no field, form, stepper,
   cancellation control, confirmation step or consequence screen for any of them.
2. Given a customer who has gone to that surface to do any of the five, when they
   come back to ReachKit — having done it, or having done nothing — then they
   return to the screen they left from and are still signed in.
3. Given a founder acting on an offer to subscribe — the report's pricing card,
   or any other price surface (REQ-021) — when they proceed to pay, then every
   step from that offer to the completed payment happens on Stripe's own checkout,
   and ReachKit presents no card field, no payment form and no billing form of its
   own at any point on that path.
4. Given an event Stripe records against a customer's billing record, when the
   customer is told about it, then the mail telling them is Stripe's and ReachKit
   sends none of its own on that occasion. Given a mail ReachKit sends, when the
   customer reads it, then the occasion it was sent on is one ReachKit's own
   records or its own clock produced — whatever payment lies behind it, and
   whatever facts Stripe also holds about it — and neither its subject nor its
   body states any value criterion 5 withholds from a ReachKit surface.
5. Given a customer who wants the date or amount of their next invoice, which
   card is on file, or what they have been invoiced before, when they go to find
   out, then no ReachKit surface states any of those values — a screen or a mail
   alike — and criterion 1's control is offered on the screen where they looked
   for them.
6. Given Stripe cannot be reached — criterion 1's billing surface refuses to open
   a session, or criterion 3's checkout cannot be produced — when the customer or
   the founder acts on the control, then they are told in writing, on the screen
   they were on, that billing cannot be reached and that they may try again;
   nothing about their plan or their account changes, a signed-in customer stays
   signed in, and no ReachKit field, form, payment step or cancellation control
   stands in its place.

## Non-goals
- Resuming after a cancellation, which REQ-076 criterion 6 owns and this
  requirement does not move: criterion 1 routes five things to Stripe and resuming
  is not one of them.
- The plan itself. There is one plan and no change to it (REQ-076's non-goal), so
  criterion 1 routes nothing about the plan to Stripe.
- What a cancelled customer keeps and until when, the paid-through date, and the
  access gate the weekly and daily loops read — REQ-076.
- The two written notices a customer with hosted pages is owed before serving
  stops: REQ-076 criterion 11 states their occasions, their count and their
  channel. Criterion 4 only settles that they are ReachKit's and not Stripe's.
- Which fields Stripe's surfaces are configured to offer and where each sits —
  REQ-076 criterion 2's non-goal already places that with the blueprint.
- The price and the plan name: €49/mo is a public product fact stated on every
  price surface (REQ-021, REQ-022 criterion 1), not a value Stripe holds about one
  customer, and criterion 5 does not reach either.
- What checkout records against the billing record — REQ-022. What a founder may
  be asked at checkout and what may be asked before payment — REQ-020 criteria 1
  and 6; criterion 3 adds only that no ReachKit-rendered payment or billing form
  stands anywhere on that path.
- The webhook that opens an account from a completed payment, and everything a
  founder who has paid is owed until they are inside — REQ-024. Provisioning is
  ReachKit's and this boundary does not move it.
- Refunds, chargebacks and failed renewals, deferred past launch by owner ruling
  (REQ-076): criterion 4 states only whose mail carries them.
- Anything on Stripe's own surfaces — their wording, layout, fields or mail.
- Which kinds of mail exist and where that register lives: REQ-064's non-goal
  assigns it to the mail blueprint, and the examples of Stripe's own mail it names
  illustrate criterion 4's class rather than bounding it — where the two differ,
  criterion 4's origin test governs. Criterion 4 draws a boundary and adds no
  kind, removes none and changes no row.

## Open questions
- [ ] REQ-076 criterion 1 promises Settings shows four things: the plan and
      price, the date and amount of the next invoice, the card on file, and a way
      to reach invoices. Criteria 1 and 5 above keep the plan and its price
      readable in ReachKit and withhold the other three, offering the control in
      their place. Which holds — Settings reads Stripe and shows the next invoice
      date and the card, or Settings shows the plan, the price and the control and
      nothing else? (owner: user)
- [ ] REQ-076 criteria 3 and 7, and BUILD §4.7's line "cancelling keeps
      everything running until {date}", put three statements on a ReachKit screen
      at the moment cancellation is confirmed: the exact date access ends, that
      the export stays open with no end date, and the day hosted pages stop being
      served. Criterion 1 leaves no such screen. Does ReachKit state those three
      before handing the customer to Stripe, or afterwards in an `account` mail,
      or does the line go? (owner: user)
