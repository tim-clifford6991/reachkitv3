# REQ-004 — The report opens with one score, its band and its drivers — and what could not be measured says so, while a measured zero stays a zero

- Priority: Must

## User story
As a founder with zero SEO knowledge reading a verdict about my own site, I want
the top of the report to tell me in one number and one word how findable I am,
and every failed measurement to be admitted rather than shown as a bad result, so
that I know where I stand and can trust every number that is shown.

## Rationale
A founder must learn where they stand from the top of the report alone — one
number, one word for what that number means, the three things behind it, and the
domain and date they belong to — and must never be shown a free verdict that has
been reduced, blurred or held back to sell them the paid one. Every number they
read, and every number the product stores, must trace to something we actually
measured: an input whose existence we could not determine is admitted as
unmeasured, an input we read that contained none of what we counted stays a zero
wherever it is shown or recorded, work the scan never got to says it was never
taken, and the three are never shown as the same thing — rendering an outage as 0
invents a customer fact and makes a false public accusation about someone's site.
This requirement is the single home for that rule, for display and for storage
alike, on every surface the report renders; the formula, its sub-measures and the
band thresholds are mechanism for the blueprint. Placement: JN-001 steps 2 and 3.
Sources: `BUILD.md` §4.1 (header strip) and States (degraded), §5 (bands;
"Identical free/paid; no tier parameter"; "Empty denominators read 0, never
null"; a driver that could not be measured is null; "generic noindex on the home
document ⇒ 0, and score ⇒ 0"), §2.5 (designed empty states) and §6.5 ("Caps
degrade (skip remaining optional work, mark scan `degraded`), never throw"),
which is why a spend ceiling reached over a driver still outstanding ends in a
"—" and not a computed score. No implementation exists to touch: this repo
carries no `src/`.

## Acceptance criteria
1. Given a completed report whose score could be computed, when it opens, then
   the first module shows a single score on a 0–100 scale, its band as one of the
   four written words — Invisible, Hard to find, Findable, Dominant — the domain
   measured, and the date it was measured.
2. Given the score, when it renders, then the three drivers it is composed of
   are shown beside it, each named and each with its own value, or with "—" in
   place of a value the scan could not measure (criterion 6).
3. Given a report whose score could not be computed (criteria 6 and 9), when it
   opens, then the first module shows "—" in place of the score, shows no band
   word at all, still names the domain and the date, and carries one written line
   saying the verdict could not be reached, naming every driver that has no
   value, and stating for each which of the two reasons applies — that the input
   behind it could not be determined (criterion 6) or that the scan stopped
   before reaching it (criterion 9) — in the words that criterion gives the case,
   and never calling a driver the scan never attempted a missing one. No band is
   stated, implied, or guessed from the drivers that were measured.
4. Given the band, when it renders, then it is conveyed in words and not by
   colour alone.
5. Given a free report, when it renders, then no part of the score, the band or
   the three drivers is hidden, blurred, rounded down, locked, or marked as
   available on payment; the free report shows the whole verdict.
6. Given an input whose existence the scan could not determine — it could not
   tell whether a gate or an input is there at all, because nothing was
   returned, the answer was unreadable, or a data source did not answer — when
   the report renders and the result is stored, then every driver depending on
   it is shown and recorded as "—", one written line names which driver was
   missing or, where no driver depended on that input, names what could not be
   measured, and the score is shown as "—" only where a driver it is composed of
   depended on that input. An input no driver depends on never withholds the
   score.
7. Given an input the scan did read that contained none of what was counted —
   including a site's access rules, read successfully, that gate nothing — when
   the report renders and the result is stored, then the value is shown as 0 with
   its denominator and recorded as a measured zero, never as "—", "unknown", a
   missing section, or a measurement that could not be taken. Access rules read
   successfully that do gate are a measured result too and are likewise never
   "—": a home document the scan read that tells every reader not to index it
   scores 0 with the band word that 0 carries, on criterion 1's terms, and never
   a "—". What the blocked-readers count then shows is REQ-009 criterion 3's.
   Reading is what divides this criterion from criterion 6.
8. Given a domain the scan measured successfully that appears in none of the
   searches measured and is cited in none of the AI answers that appeared, when
   its numbers are stored, then that absence is recorded as a measured zero,
   never as a measurement that could not be taken.
9. Given a measurement the scan never attempted because it stopped early — at
   the 90-second ceiling (REQ-003 criterion 5) or the free path's spend ceiling
   (REQ-003 criterion 11) — when the report renders and the result is
   stored, then everything depending on it is shown and recorded as "—", one
   written line states it was not measured because the scan stopped early, and it
   is never shown or recorded as 0. Where a driver the score is composed of was
   among the work cut off — including a driver whose measurement was still
   outstanding when the spend ceiling was reached — the score itself is shown as
   "—" on the terms criterion 3 states, never computed, banded or estimated from
   the drivers that were measured; where no driver was cut off, the score still
   stands.
10. Given a section whose data could not be retrieved, when the report renders,
    then that section is absent and one written line names the missing section
    and states that it could not be measured — never an empty card, a persistent
    spinner, or placeholder content.
11. Given a partially failed scan, when the report renders, then every part that
    was measured is shown in full and the report is otherwise usable.
12. Given a measurement this scan could not take — an input whose existence it
    could not determine (criterion 6) or work it never attempted (criterion 9) —
    when the report renders and the result is stored, then nothing stands in its
    place: no estimate, no interpolated value, no default, and no value carried
    over from an earlier scan of that domain. Every number the report shows and
    the product stores comes from the scan whose measurement date the report
    carries.

## Non-goals
- No second score, sub-score dashboard, or per-driver drill-down on the free
  report.
- No competitor score, grade letter, or ranking of the customer against a
  cohort.
- No explanation of the formula on screen; the report explains meaning, not
  arithmetic.
- No estimated, interpolated, or last-known-good value substituted for a failed
  measurement — stated as criterion 12, not left to this list.
- Whether any of these written lines may be model-written rather than written by
  us: REQ-093 is the single home for that rule and it holds here unchanged.
- No hiding of the whole report because one part failed.
- What a read access rule makes of the blocked-readers count — REQ-009 holds
  that; criteria 6 to 12 hold only whether the result is a measured value or a
  "—", and criterion 7 the one case where a read gate settles the score itself.
- The exhaustive classification of every scan outcome into one of these cases,
  and which driver each input feeds: the blueprint's, derived from this
  requirement's promise that an unmeasured input is never shown as a zero and a
  measured zero is never shown as unmeasured.
