# REQ-021 — Buying with no report behind the purchase

- Priority: Must

## User story
As a founder who reached a price surface without running a report, I want to
buy and start anyway, so that I do not have to scan my site first to become a
customer.

## Rationale
Not every buyer arrives from their own report — some come from a shared link, a
pricing page or a conversation — and refusing them, or inventing a report they
never ran, are both worse than asking one extra question. What must be true for
this customer: a place to buy exists away from any report, the purchase
completes on identical terms, the site address the product does not hold is
asked at setup rather than guessed, an address it cannot reach is caught there
rather than after the money has moved, and nothing is presented as measured for
a domain nobody has measured (`BUILD.md` §13, §4.3). Whether a purchase has
a report behind it is a fact about the purchase — whether a report carried into
its checkout (REQ-090 criterion 3) — never about what the buyer has run before,
so a founder who ran a report and then bought from a price surface is a buyer
with no report behind their purchase and is asked for their site address like
any other. An address accepted at setup is not a life sentence: Settings offers
the control that changes the site's domain afterwards (REQ-070 criterion 1),
and what that change then measures, and when, is REQ-071's (criterion 9 — at
the next weekly re-measurement, never on save), so criterion 5's refusal of an
address the product cannot reach shuts no door on a founder who typed a real
domain that is not theirs. A founder it does refuse is never trapped by the
refusal: Settings, cancellation and export stay open to them with setup
unfinished (REQ-025 criterion 5; REQ-076 criterion 3).

## Acceptance criteria
1. Given a founder on a price surface with no report behind it, when they buy,
   then the purchase completes on the same terms as one made from a report.
2. Given a purchase made with no report behind it, when the founder reaches
   setup, then setup asks for their site address before its other decisions and
   does not proceed without one, and the field is empty: no domain taken from
   the address they paid with, or from their payment details, is pre-filled or
   offered to them as their site.
3. Given a purchase made with no report behind it, when the founder is at setup
   or in the app before their site has been measured, then no score, report or
   question result is shown for their domain and nothing on screen is presented
   as a measurement of it; what is offered from the market they stated —
   suggested rivals among it — is not a measurement of their site and remains
   available (REQ-027 criterion 1).
4. Given the founder has given their site address, when they submit setup, then
   the deep pass runs against that domain and the account behaves from then on
   exactly like one bought from a report.
5. Given a founder entering their site address at setup, when the address is
   not one the product can reach — reaching being that the address is a domain
   name that resolves in DNS, the same test a rival domain meets (REQ-027
   criterion 2), so a site that is new, thin, unbuilt, coming-soon or blocking
   our fetcher is reached and accepted — then setup says so in one written line
   and does not accept it until an address it can reach is given.
6. Given a surface that offers ReachKit away from any report, when a founder
   reaches it, then it carries exactly one offer to subscribe, states the price
   on the terms REQ-022 criterion 1 fixes and what the subscription does on the
   same terms the offer at the end of a report states (REQ-090 criterion 2), and
   one control on it begins checkout with no account, sign-in, password or form
   asked first (REQ-020 criterion 1).
7. Given a founder who has paid and whose own site address setup refuses
   (criterion 5), when they are refused, then the same written line names one
   way to reach a person (REQ-024 criterion 5) and tells them they can cancel
   without finishing setup; the address they typed and every answer they have
   already given are still there when they return; and nothing about their
   account requires setup to be finished before they can reach Settings, cancel
   or export their content (REQ-025 criterion 5; REQ-070 criterion 2; REQ-076
   criterion 3) — so
   a founder this refusal stops can always either get help or leave.

## Non-goals
- Guessing the domain from the buyer's email address or payment details —
  criterion 2 carries the customer's promise that nothing is pre-filled.
- Asking for the domain during checkout — REQ-020 criterion 6 fixes what
  checkout may ask.
- Running a free report on the buyer's behalf before setup; criterion 3 carries
  what the founder must not be shown until their own site is measured.
- More than one site per account.
- A second plan, tier, trial or discount on the surface criterion 6 requires
  (REQ-022 criterion 3).
- Changing the site's domain after setup, and the Settings surface that does it
  — REQ-070's and REQ-071's, cited in the rationale, not restated here.
- A person setting a site address as the ordinary way anyone reaches setup:
  criterion 7 is the way out for the founder criterion 5 refuses, not a route
  offered to buyers whose address is accepted.
- How quickly the person criterion 7 names settles the address, and whether that
  route ends in a finished setup at all: settling it is manual work outside the
  product, on which no criterion here can be failed. What the founder is owed
  while it is open is criterion 7's, and leaving without waiting for it is
  REQ-076 criterion 3's.
- Anything else the surface criterion 6 requires may carry, and how the terms it
  states are laid out — the blueprint that owns that surface decides both;
  criterion 6 asks only that the buyer there is told what a buyer at the end of
  a report is told.
