## The score's name and scale, checked

The screenshot says "Discoverability Score" and "/100". Grep of the whole corpus
for `Discoverability|findability score` returns no requirement, no blueprint and
no journey giving the number a customer-visible name. What the corpus does fix:

- REQ-004 criterion 1, verbatim: "the first module shows a single score on a
  0–100 scale, its band as one of the four written words — Invisible, Hard to
  find, Findable, Dominant".
- `BUILD.md` §5, verbatim: "Bands: 0–24 Invisible · 25–49 Hard to find · 50–74
  Findable · 75–100 Dominant".

So the **scale agrees** ("/100" is REQ-004's 0–100), the **band word agrees**
("Hard to find" is one of the four, and 47 falls in 25–49), and the specimen is
internally consistent with the corpus. Only the **name** is new, and a name a
customer reads is the owner's, not the system's — hence REQ-098's first open
question rather than a resolution here.

## Why criterion 5 is derivable and the specimen's provenance is not

`00-project.md` standing law 5, verbatim, with the source's own emphasis and no
other: "**One claim, one home.** Every constant — price, cap, limit, copy string —
is defined once. Every UI number traces to a stored measurement with a date."
REQ-004 criterion 12, verbatim: "nothing stands in its place: no estimate, no
interpolated value, no default … Every number the report shows and the product
stores comes from the scan whose measurement date the report carries." Between
them, a figure on any ReachKit surface is either a measurement with its date or
must not read as one — that is criterion 5, derived, not asked. Which of the
owner's options the panel actually is (real scan · fixed example) is a
customer-visible claim about a named domain, so it is asked.

Three things in criterion 5 are chosen rather than transcribed, under rule 1.1,
because "unmistakably not a measurement" had no observable test and nobody to
judge it:

1. **A reserved example domain**, so the second branch has a mechanical test — a
   domain that resolves to no real site (the RFC 2606 reserved set) can be
   asserted against a list; "unmistakable" cannot. Cost of reversing: one clause
   and one string.
2. **A written line on the card saying it is an example** — the words are the
   owner's and are owed (open question 3, item 6); that a line exists is the
   testable part and is this criterion's.
3. **No identifiable customer's domain, score or band on this screen at all.**
   Derived from REQ-020 criterion 5 — "nothing is said about whether any account
   or payment exists until an address has been given" — applied to the panel
   rather than only to the form: a screen that renders with no session cannot
   carry one customer's measurements where any stranger reads them. This is a
   privacy floor, not a promise change, so it is derived rather than asked (rule
   1.3).

The pill "+6 pts est." fits neither branch as transcribed, and that is the whole
of open question 2: an estimated gain is not a stored measurement and is not
offered as fiction. The requirement does not resolve it, because which of the two
things gives way — the pill, or the real scan — is a customer-visible claim.

## The destination of "Start a free scan →", derived not transcribed

The transcription records only "the second half an accent link"; the owner
endorsed the string, not a target. Criterion 2 states the landing page as the
destination, and that is this analyst's derivation under rule 1.1, recorded here
rather than presented as the owner's: REQ-001 criterion 1 puts the one text input
that starts a free scan on the landing page and nowhere else, so a link labelled
"Start a free scan" has exactly one place in this product to reach. Cost of
reversing: one clause in criterion 2.
