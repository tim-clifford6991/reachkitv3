## Resuming: removed from criterion 1, and why

The first draft routed resuming to Stripe with the rest, on the owner's "all
billing … entirely". Reversed at review, on two verified facts rather than a
preference:

- **The portal cannot do it.** BP-060's `PORTAL_FEATURES` sets
  `subscription_update: { enabled: false }`, and a resume after the paid-through
  date needs a new subscription rather than a portal edit. Of the six things the
  first draft routed to Stripe, five are covered by the four enabled keys —
  `payment_method_update`, `customer_update` with
  `allowed_updates: ['address', 'tax_id', 'email']`, `invoice_history` and
  `subscription_cancel` — and resuming is the only one unreached. The `rests-on`
  row's first draft said "four of the six", which read as two unreached and named
  neither; it now names the coverage key by key.
- **Resuming is not only a Stripe act.** BP-060's `resumeSubscription` performs
  ReachKit-side effects Stripe's surface cannot: it "advances `paid_through`,
  clears `hosted_serving_ends_at` and both notice stamps". Moving the control
  without moving those effects would leave REQ-076 c6's self-service promise with
  no surface and BP-060's own function with no caller.

So resuming stays where approved REQ-076 c6, BP-055's `ACTIONS` member `'resume'`
and JN-004's last step already put it, and REQ-097 records it as a non-goal. Cost
of reversing: one clause in criterion 1 and one non-goal line. Consequences: the
`rests-on` row narrows to the `tax_id` leg alone; `resumeSubscription` is no
longer contradicted; and JN-004 step 5 needs no resume clause, which is why the
step is left as it stands (rule 5.7 is met by the step that already exercises
REQ-076).

## "The plan": removed from criterion 1, and why

The first draft listed the plan among the things one control reaches on Stripe's
surface. `PORTAL_FEATURES` sets `subscription_update: { enabled: false }` and
REQ-076's non-goal fixes "one plan at €49/mo, with no upgrades, downgrades, annual
billing, seats or add-ons", so that control led to a surface on which nothing
about the plan could be done. Criterion 1 no longer routes it; the plan and its
price stay readable in ReachKit under this requirement's own price non-goal, and
open question 1 now asks about the three withheld values only, not about the plan
and price it keeps.

## REQ-070 is not contradicted, and the edge moved

The first draft carried `blocked-by: REQ-070` on the reading that its criteria 1
and 2 "list billing details, invoices, and cancelling or resuming among what
Settings offers". They do not require them. Both are **ceilings**: c1 — "nothing
beyond this list is offered for them to change"; c2 — "nothing beyond this list is
offered". Criterion 1 offers strictly less than either permits: a merged control
reaching invoices and cancelling offers items that are *on* c2's list, not items
beyond it, and rendering no field for billing details satisfies a ceiling on what
may be changed. REQ-070 therefore moves to `depends-on`. Cost of reversing: one
front-matter entry. The contradiction the first draft mislabelled as REQ-070's is
really BP-055's `ACTIONS` tuple, which the front-matter now names directly.

## One control: kind, not instance

Criterion 1's first draft read "exactly one control is offered for all of it",
which contradicted criterion 4's requirement that the control appear "on the
screen where they looked": on the instance reading a second screen needed a second
instance the first clause forbade, and no test passed both. The criterion now
fixes **one destination and no per-item control**, which is what the boundary
actually needs, and leaves the number of places the control appears to criterion
5's screen-where-they-looked rule. Both are now mechanically checkable: count of
ReachKit-rendered billing surfaces is zero, and every billing control's
destination resolves to the same portal session.

## The return, and where it is promised

Criterion 1's first draft moved cancelling to Stripe and said nothing about the
customer's return. REQ-076 c2 carries the corpus's only return promise — "reach
Stripe's own customer portal and return to Settings afterwards still signed in" —
and scopes it to its own four acts, which do not include cancelling. JN-004 step 5
already promises it for cancelling: "leaving ReachKit for Stripe's own billing
surface to do any of the three, and coming back still signed in". Criterion 2 is
that promise, widened from four acts to five, so no build satisfies the boundary
while ending a cancelling customer's session. Derived from the journey and REQ-076
c2 rather than asked (rule 1.3); reversal is one criterion.

## The boundary criterion 4 draws, and why it is drawn by occasion

Three boundary tests were candidates and only the third survives, because the
corpus has cases that split the first two.

- **"Anything that happens to a customer's money."** Refuted by REQ-024 c3: a
  founder whose second purchase was charged is told, in an `account` mail, that it
  bought no second subscription. Money moved; Stripe cannot say that sentence.
- **"Stripe holds no fact about it."** Refuted by REQ-076 c3: Stripe holds the
  period-end date, and no money moves at the moment the customer is told it.

- **"The mail's subject."** Refuted at review, and recorded because the first
  draft of criterion 3 shipped it: a subject is fixed by nothing mechanical — not
  the register, not the kind, not the trigger — so REQ-076 c11's notice on a
  lapsed renewal reads as the payment to one reader and as the hosting to another,
  and a test would need a human ruling per mail. It also governs a subject only,
  so an `account` mail whose *body* states the next invoice amount, or that the
  last renewal failed, passed it and broke the user story.

The rule criterion 4 now states is **the occasion**, and it is mechanical because
every occasion in this corpus has exactly one producer. A mail sent because Stripe
recorded an event against the customer's billing record is Stripe's; a mail sent
because a ReachKit record or the ReachKit clock produced the occasion is
ReachKit's, whatever payment lies behind it. Checked row by row against BP-016's
`MAIL_KINDS`: the `account` kind's occasions are a second purchase that bought no
subscription (REQ-024 c3), the provisioning backstops (REQ-024 c5, c6), a
destination that stopped working (REQ-074), a moved sign-in address (REQ-077 c3),
the last mail after erasure (REQ-079), and the two hosting notices (REQ-076 c11) —
whose trigger is `paid_through` passing on ReachKit's own clock (BP-060, verbatim:
"`hasActiveAccess` is `paid_through > now()`, full stop"), not a Stripe event.
Every one is ReachKit's, decided without a reader ruling on a subject. Criterion
4's second sentence binds the body as well as the subject, closing the gap the
subject test left, and criterion 5 now says "a screen or a mail alike" so the
withheld values cannot re-enter through a mail body.

**The enumeration is gone, deliberately.** The first draft listed five Stripe
occasions where REQ-064's non-goal lists three — two copies of one boundary,
already differing in two entries (rule 2.4). Criterion 4 names the class by its
producer and lists nothing; REQ-097's non-goal records that REQ-064's list
illustrates that class rather than bounding it, and that the origin test governs
where they differ.

The superseded subject rule, kept for the record: a mail whose
subject is the payment itself is Stripe's; a mail whose subject is the account or
what ReachKit does for the customer is ReachKit's, whatever payment prompted it
and whatever facts Stripe also holds. That decides both cases above — REQ-024 c3
is ReachKit's, REQ-076 c3 and c11 are ReachKit's, a receipt or a card-expiry
warning is Stripe's — and it is the reading the owner's own sentence carries:
"stripe handles all billing notifications and emails".

## The mail register, checked

The owner asked which mail kinds disappear and whether the count changes. **None,
and no.** The register is not REQ-064's: REQ-064's non-goal assigns it to "one
table the mail blueprint holds", and BP-016 decision 1 makes it a typed constant
(`blueprints/BP-016.md`, `MAIL_KINDS`). REQ-064's non-goal names eight kinds; the
ninth, `first-page-unavailable`, is BP-016 decision 2's. **REQ-097 states neither
the count nor the list anywhere** — BP-016's own open-questions note records this
same restatement being cleaned out of REQ-079, REQ-074 and REQ-077, and it is not
re-minted here.

What the check found, recorded once, here: no row in `MAIL_KINDS` is a payment
notification. The only row the statement comes near is `account`, whose
`occasionsFrom` reads `'REQ-024, REQ-074, REQ-076, REQ-077, REQ-079'` — and by
criterion 3's subject test every one of those five occasions is an account,
hosting, destination or identity fact rather than a payment: a second purchase
that bought no subscription (REQ-024 c3), the 15-minute and 24-hour provisioning
backstops (REQ-024 c5, c6), a destination that stopped working (REQ-074), the day
hosted pages stop being served (REQ-076 c11), a moved sign-in address (REQ-077
c3), and the last mail after erasure (REQ-079). The register is unchanged, which
is why REQ-097 carries it as a non-goal rather than a criterion.

## The price, checked rather than asked

Criterion 4 enumerates what no ReachKit surface may state and the plan's price is
not in it. Derivation (rule 1.3, so it is not a question): €49/mo is a public
product fact stated on every price surface — REQ-022 criterion 1 binds "any
surface that states the price" and REQ-021 places the offer — not a value Stripe
holds about one customer, and it cannot go stale against Stripe the way a next
invoice date can. A signed-in customer may therefore still read €49 in ReachKit.
Cost of reversing: one clause in criterion 4 and one non-goal.
