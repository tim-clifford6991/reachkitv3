Why this sits here and not in the requirement: the vendor tier, the per-call
price and the scan's cost cap are implementation and cost-control facts (rule
2.1 — a REQ carries no vendor evidence). REQ-003 states only the observable
promise, the sixty seconds; live mode is the mechanism bought to keep it, and
`CAP_FREE` is the blueprint's constraint, not a customer-visible criterion.

## The 90-second hard ceiling (derived, rule 1.1)

REQ-003 criterion 5 stops measuring at 90 seconds. `BUILD.md` fixes 60 seconds
and no ceiling above it, so the ceiling is a derived parameter, not a
transcribed one.

Derivation: 60 seconds is the promise a visitor is held to (§11, "Free ≈60s
live"), and a ceiling equal to the promise would cut off any run that overran by
a single slow vendor call — turning a report that was one call from complete
into a truncated one. A ceiling of 90 seconds is a 50% overrun: it leaves room
for exactly one vendor call to answer slowly past the promise while still
bounding what any visitor waits, and it is short enough that the wait stays
inside the "abandons an unlabelled wait" window the requirement is written
against. The upper bound is behavioural, not technical — every second past the
promise is a second the visitor already believes the page is stuck.

Cost of reversing it: low. The ceiling is one pinned constant
(`00-project.md`, "`src/lib/config/constants.ts` | **Every** pinned number in
the specification", asserted by `tests/pins.test.ts`), and moving it changes one
criterion's number and one pin — no schema, no vendor contract, no
customer-visible string beyond the written line REQ-003 criterion 5 already
requires.

## The window criterion 2 counts over (derived, rule 1.1)

`BUILD.md` §11 fixes "Free ≈60s live" and names no population or window, so the
sample criterion 2 is judged over is a derived parameter. Chosen: any 100
consecutive free scans of reachable domains, in start order, with a budget of 5
overruns. Derivation: 100 consecutive scans is the smallest window in which a
5% budget is a whole number of scans, so a verdict never turns on rounding; it
is counted in start order rather than per calendar period so a low-traffic day
cannot pass the criterion on a sample of three scans, and a launch build can be
judged the moment it has served 100 scans rather than waiting out a month.
"Reachable" excludes the domains criterion 4's failure path owns, whose duration
is bounded by criterion 5 and not by this one.

Cost of reversing it: low — the window and the budget are two pinned constants
in the test harness, not product behaviour; no customer-visible string, schema
or vendor contract moves with them.

## The correction's spend ceiling (transcribed)

- §6.7 step 5, verbatim: "one correction per free scan re-runs steps 2–5
  (~4.2¢; worst case 10.5¢, still under the 12¢ cap)."

The clause costs the correction inside the free scan's one 12¢ cap, which is why
REQ-003 criterion 11 states that a correction opens no second ceiling. Nothing
is derived here — the shared ceiling is the source's own arithmetic.
